the night

one time, one button

Set when you need to be up by, and start the night. White noise carries you to sleep and holds through the small hours, then fades to silence. After a quiet gap, yolo listens — a 30-second statistical calibration of your room, then a trigger set a few standard deviations above its baseline, so distant traffic never wakes you but the rustle of you naturally stirring does. Miss the window, and the alarm sounds at your "up by" time no matter what.

the face

no clock — a sky

The night screen shows nothing that tells the time. The background is near-black until about ninety minutes before your wake, then warms through pre-dawn to a sunrise — one glance answers "is it time yet."

The two rings are the real sun and moon: each ring is that body's complete daily lap around you, and the share of a ring above the horizon line is exactly the share of the day it spends in the sky. The sun's ring sweeps high to low once a year with the seasons; the moon's makes the same sweep every 27 days, and a full moon always rides opposite the sun. The bodies sit at their true positions, computed on-device — when the moon touches the line on screen, it's rising or setting outside your window.

the build

developed in the open

yolo alarm is open source — the code, the design decisions, and the spec that drove them live in the repository. The commit history is the development story: the merge of a white-noise app and a smart alarm into one night, the death of every clock on the night screen, and the astronomy that replaced them.

Built with Swift and SwiftUI. On iOS 26 and later, a system AlarmKit alarm backstops every session, so you can't oversleep even if the app is closed overnight.

privacy policy

everything stays on your phone

What we collect: nothing. yolo alarm does not collect, store, or transmit any personal data. No accounts, no analytics, no tracking, no advertising, no servers.

Microphone. The microphone is used solely to detect the sounds of you stirring, and only evaluated during your wake window. Audio is processed on your device in real time — nothing is ever recorded, stored, or sent anywhere. In no-alarm mode the microphone is never activated at all.

Location. Your approximate location is used only to place the real sun and moon on the night screen. It is computed against on-device astronomy and never leaves your phone. Decline it and the app works the same, minus the sky.

Storage. Your settings live locally on your device. Nothing leaves your phone.

Contact. For support or questions, open an issue on GitHub.
